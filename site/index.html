One night, from your phone’s side and from your Mac’s.

22:30

  1. Your phone locks, and the app following your sensor goes to sleep with it.
  2. LibreLinkUp keeps one reading every 15 minutes. The minutes in between are gone, and the dip just after two goes with them.
  3. CGM Relay asked every minute from your Mac, all night.
  4. Open your app whenever you like. It catches up on every minute, and xDrip4iOS writes them all to Apple Health.

Let your phone sleep.

To get every minute on its own, a follower app has to stay open, or keep itself running in the background in a way that drains your battery. Let your phone put it to sleep and the minutes are lost, and LibreLinkUp only gives back one reading every 15.

CGM Relay runs on your Mac instead and keeps every minute, so your app can stay closed all night. Open xDrip4iOS, xDrip+, LoopFollow or Nightguard whenever you like, and it catches up on all of it, minute by minute.

That’s what fills in your health data too. xDrip4iOS writes every minute it catches up on to Apple Health, so the night is there in full even though nothing was running on your phone. On Android, xDrip+ can write its readings to Health Connect.

To your app, CGM Relay looks like a Nightscout site, so there’s nothing new to learn on your phone.

Set up in a couple of minutes

The first time it opens, CGM Relay walks you through four steps.

  1. Sign in

    With your LibreLinkUp email and password, checked with LibreLinkUp before anything is saved.

  2. Reach your phone

    With Tailscale, one click gives your phone a private HTTPS address that works at home and away. Your own network works too.

  3. Connect your app

    Scan a QR code from xDrip+ or LoopFollow, or open a one-time pairing page on your phone for xDrip4iOS and Nightguard.

  4. Open at Login

    So it’s back on its own after your Mac restarts. Then it lives in your menu bar.

Quiet until something needs you

The icon stays monochrome while readings are fresh, like the rest of your menu bar. It turns amber when a reading is late and red when something stops, and the menu says what to do about it. If you want the number up there all the time, turn on “Show the reading in the menu bar”.

Works with the app you already use

Anything that follows a Nightscout site can follow CGM Relay. Setup gives you the address and an access token; this is where they go.

AppWhere the address and token go
xDrip4iOSIn Settings, Follower mode with Nightscout as the data source, then Sharing and Services, Nightscout: the address in URL, any port it has in Port, the token in Token, and API_SECRET left blank.
xDrip+ AndroidIn Settings, Nightscout Follower as the hardware data source, then the address with the token in front of it in Nightscout Follow URL.
LoopFollowIn Settings, Nightscout: the address in URL and the token in Token.
NightguardIn Preferences, one Nightscout URL field: the address, then ?token= and the token.

Only one thing should follow your LibreLinkUp account. Once your app reads from CGM Relay, switch its own LibreLinkUp follower off.

Your data stays yours

Readings are stored encrypted on your Mac, and only apps holding your access token can read them. Nothing can reach your Mac from outside until you choose how. There are no accounts, analytics or cloud of ours in between.

LibreLinkUp
Your own account, to fetch your readings.
GitHub
Once a day, to check for a newer version. It carries the app’s name and version and your internet address, nothing about you, your account or your readings, and you can turn it off in Settings.
Your phone
Your follower app talks to your Mac, not to us.
Tailscale
Only when you click the button that turns it on.

Questions

Something else? Ask on GitHub.

Which sensors does it work with?

Any FreeStyle Libre sensor that shares its readings through LibreLinkUp. If someone can follow you in the LibreLinkUp app, CGM Relay can follow you too.

Does my Mac have to stay on?

Yes. CGM Relay can only ask for readings while it runs, so it needs a Mac that stays awake, or a small server running it instead. With Open at Login on, it starts again by itself after a restart, and LibreLinkUp’s 15-minute history fills in the gap while it was off.

I use Android. Does it work?

Yes, through xDrip+. Choose Nightscout Follower as its hardware data source and put the address CGM Relay gives you, with the token in front of it, in Nightscout Follow URL, or scan the QR code setup shows you. The relay itself runs on your Mac.

What does it cost?

Nothing. It’s free and open source, and there’s no account to create and no cloud of ours to pay for.

How long does it keep readings?

15 days by default, and you can change that in Settings. They’re kept encrypted on your Mac, so a copied disk or a backup shows only timestamps.

What if my phone loses its connection?

CGM Relay sees only what LibreLinkUp has. If the phone running LibreLink is offline, those minutes reach LibreLinkUp, and then CGM Relay, later as 15-minute history.

Could it stop working one day?

LibreLinkUp has no official public API. CGM Relay uses the same endpoints the LibreLinkUp app and other open source followers use, and those can change. It recovers by itself from LibreLinkUp’s rate limits and outages.

Can I make treatment decisions from it?

No. CGM Relay is not a medical device. Readings and alerts can arrive late, go missing or be wrong, so don’t make treatment decisions from it, and keep the official Libre app and its alarms on for lows.
